## 1. A-MAC — Adaptive Memory Admission Control for LLM Agents

- **arXiv:** 2603.04549
- **Authors:** Zhang, Jiang, Wang, Behr, Zhao, Friedman, Chu, Anoun
- **Status:** ✓ real, title matches, content verified

### What the paper actually says

Frames memory admission as a **structured decision problem** with
five interpretable factors that merken hasn't named explicitly:

1. **Future utility** — probability this event will be useful later
2. **Factual confidence** — how sure are we the event is accurate
3. **Semantic novelty** — does this add new information
4. **Temporal recency** — when did it happen
5. **Content type prior** — what *kind* of content this is

Approach is **hybrid**: lightweight rule-based feature extraction
+ a single LLM-assisted utility assessment + cross-validated
optimization. Not pure-rule like merken, not pure-LLM like Mem0 /
A-mem.

### Results

- F1 = 0.583 on LoCoMo
- 31% latency reduction vs LLM-native baselines
- **Content type prior is the single most influential factor**

### What the reading list claimed that ISN'T in the paper

> *"critica que los enfoques LLM-native como A-mem y Mem0 tienen
> overhead computacional alto y limitada interpretabilidad"*

The A-MAC paper **does not explicitly critique A-mem or Mem0 by
name**, at least not in the sections I could fetch. The broader
framing of "LLM-native approaches have high overhead and limited
interpretability" is implicit in the paper's motivation but is
not a direct named critique.

Not a big deal — the framing is directionally correct — but
worth knowing before anyone quotes this paper in a slide as
"A-MAC calls out Mem0."

### Relevance to merken

**High.** A-MAC is the closest published system to merken's own
approach, and its empirical framing is the one we should adopt
for comparisons.

Two concrete ideas worth adopting:

- **Content type prior is a factor merken's HeuristicWriteDecider
  does not currently model.** We have `empty`, `too_short`,
  `too_long`, `dup_exact` — all mechanical checks. A "content
  type prior" would check *what kind* of content this is
  (decision? observation? tool-call echo? user correction?
  ambient chat?) and adjust the admission threshold accordingly.
  A decision about architecture has high prior, a "ok thanks"
  has low prior. This is implementable as a tags-based policy
  without adding LLM calls.

- **F1 = 0.583 on LoCoMo is the number merken should target.**
  merken has no LoCoMo number yet (Phase B of
  BENCHMARK_STRATEGY.md). Until it does, we can't claim parity,
  above, or below. But A-MAC gives us a specific benchmark
  target to compete against the next time we build a retrieval
  benchmark runner.

**Concrete next slice idea:** A `ContentTypePriorDecider` that
extends HeuristicWriteDecider with a small lookup table:

```python
_PRIORS = {
    "decision": 0.9,      # high prior, keep unless obviously dup
    "observation": 0.6,
    "tool_call_echo": 0.1,
    "ambient_chat": 0.2,
}
```

Routing by tag: the caller tags the event with a content type
(or we parse it from role/content), the decider multiplies the
prior into its "novel" confidence score. **Not urgent** — ships
only if loop_quality shows merken misclassifies events.

## 2. CMA — Continuum Memory Architectures

- **arXiv:** 2601.09913
- **Author:** Joe Logan (single author)
- **Status:** ✓ real, content matches most of the claim

### What the paper actually says

Defines **Continuum Memory Architecture (CMA)** as a class of
systems maintaining internal state across interactions through:

- Persistent state management (information evolves, not
  static)
- Selective retention
- Associative routing
- Temporal chaining
- Consolidation into higher-order abstractions

Framing is **architectural**, not method-specific. The paper
argues retrieval-augmented generation is insufficient because
it's stateless; CMA is the alternative.

### What the reading list claimed

> *"Propone consolidation como background process con replay,
> abstracción, y extracción de gist"*

The paper mentions consolidation and abstraction. It does **not**
explicitly use the terms "replay" or "gist extraction." The
reading-list summary is embellished — those are specific
technical terms the paper avoids, probably because it's more
manifesto than method.

Single author on arXiv. Probably not peer-reviewed. Treat as a
framing reference, not a source of novel algorithms.

### Relevance to merken

**Medium for framing, low for concrete ideas.**

Validates that merken's layered model (episodic → semantic, with
forget as selective retention) aligns with a published
architectural direction. "Continuum memory" is a reasonable
umbrella term for what merken does.

**One idea worth stealing:** *temporal chaining*. merken stores
`added_at` on every doc but does nothing with it at recall time.
A `TemporalRecaller` that prefers recent events on
time-sensitive queries ("what did we decide yesterday?") could
be built without a new memory layer — just a different
should_recall policy that reads `added_at` and re-ranks. **Not
urgent** — ships if a scenario demonstrates the value.

## 3. TiMem — Temporal-Hierarchical Memory Consolidation

- **arXiv:** 2601.02845
- **Authors:** Li, Yu, Ni, Zeng, Xu, Zhang, Li, Sang, Duan,
  Wang, Liu, Tan (12 authors, probably CN institutions)
- **Status:** ✓ real, title and description accurate

### What the paper actually says

Introduces **Temporal Memory Tree (TMT)** — a hierarchical data
structure where conversational events are organized by time
AND by abstraction level. Contributions:

1. Temporal-hierarchical organization via TMT
2. Semantic-guided consolidation across hierarchical levels
   **without fine-tuning**
3. **Complexity-aware memory recall** balancing precision and
   efficiency

The "no fine-tuning" claim is significant — they're doing the
consolidation with a frozen LLM, not training a memory head.

### Relevance to merken

**Medium.** TiMem is more structured than merken — multiple
hierarchy levels, not just two. merken's layered model
(episodic/semantic) is a flat two-level case of TMT's general
tree.

**Two ideas worth considering:**

- **Multi-level hierarchy is probably not worth adding to merken
  v1.** Two levels are enough for the loop_quality scenarios.
  Adding moments/sessions/topics/abstractions as a tree is a
  large architectural change with unclear benefit on our
  current bar. **Skip.**

- **"Complexity-aware recall" is the query-type-aware
  `should_recall` variant we deferred.** TiMem validates the
  direction without giving us a specific recipe. The idea: a
  "how many facts did we decide about X?" query is a different
  shape than "what did we do on Tuesday?" and should route to
  different levels of the hierarchy. For merken's two-level
  case, this maps to: entity/detail queries → episodic first,
  summary/theme queries → semantic first. Already on our
  backlog from earlier in the session. **TiMem is citation
  support for this direction**, not new engineering guidance.

taxonomy** and examines **5 mechanism families**. The taxonomy
and family names are not in the abstract — they're in the body
of the paper.

> *"Memory — the ability to persist, organize, and selectively

This is a meaningful difference. Jay's reading list presented
this as a direct quote supporting merken's entire premise. The
paper may or may not actually make that claim — a full read of
the paper body would resolve it. Until then, **do not cite this

## 5. SuperLocalMemory V3.3

- **arXiv:** 2604.04514
- **Author:** Varun Pratap Bhardwaj (single author)
- **Status:** ✓ real, content matches claim

### What the paper actually says

Local-first memory system with five contributions:

1. **FRQAD metric** — distance on Gaussian statistical manifold,
   differentiates high-fidelity embeddings from quantized ones
2. **Ebbinghaus adaptive forgetting** — forgetting curves coupled
   to progressive embedding compression
3. **7-channel cognitive retrieval** — semantic, keyword, entity
   graph, temporal, spreading activation, consolidation,
   Hopfield associative
4. **Long-term implicit memory** via soft prompts
5. **Auto-cognitive pipeline** — automates lifecycle

**Performance: 70.4% on LoCoMo in zero-LLM mode.**

### Jay's read

> *"Ambicioso (quizás overengineered con 60 MCP tools)"*

**Correct read.** Single author, lots of named mechanisms, five
contributions in one paper — this is the mempalace of LoCoMo.
Probably not peer-reviewed. The 70.4% on LoCoMo is the number
that actually matters; everything else is architectural
opinion.

### Relevance to merken

**One big idea worth taking seriously: Ebbinghaus decay.**

merken's ForgetConsolidated is a binary decision: either the
event is tombstoned or it isn't. Ebbinghaus decay gives each
memory a continuous **strength** that decays over time,
refreshed by access. A memory that's been recently recalled has
high strength; a memory that hasn't been touched in a month has
low strength. When strength drops below a threshold, forget.

This is structurally different from ForgetConsolidated. It
forgets by *disuse*, not by *coverage*. The two are
complementary:

- **ForgetConsolidated**: forget episodic events already
  preserved in a semantic fact.
- **EbbinghausDecayForget** (hypothetical): forget episodic
  events that haven't been recalled in N days AND have low
  update/access frequency.

The second would require merken to track access frequency on
episodic events. vstash's `chunks.access_count` and
`last_accessed_at` exist but aren't exposed via `DocumentInfo`
(related to vstash#172). So this would require either an merken
table that tracks access itself, or an upstream vstash feature.

**Concrete next slice:** not urgent. Only worth building if a
longitudinal scenario shows merken accumulating stale episodic
that ForgetConsolidated can't clear. Until then, math decay is
a nice-to-have. **File as a decider variant to revisit.**

### Things to NOT take

- 7-channel retrieval is overkill. merken has 2 layers +
  interleave. More channels is not a regression on our bar.
- Soft prompts / implicit memory via parameterized storage is
  a different paradigm (fine-tuning-adjacent). Out of scope.
- "Auto-cognitive pipeline" is Silt-red-flag territory — the
  "set it and let it sort itself out" pattern that mempalace
  also had. merken's explicit decision primitives are the
  opposite of this. Stay opposite.

### Target for the benchmark roadmap

**70.4% on LoCoMo in zero-LLM mode is a real, published number
we can compete against.** Combined with A-MAC's F1=0.583, the
LoCoMo landscape has two recent entrants we'd be measuring
against. Phase B of BENCHMARK_STRATEGY.md just got more
concrete: the table would include rows for
`merken-heuristic`, `merken-with-ebbinghaus` (if we build it),
A-MAC (cited claim), SuperLocalMemory (cited claim).

## 6. Agent-Memory-Paper-List (Shichun-Liu)

- **Repo:** github.com/Shichun-Liu/Agent-Memory-Paper-List
- **Status:** ✓ real, actively maintained

### Structure

3-dimensional taxonomy:

- **Forms** (storage medium): Token-level / Parametric / Latent
- **Functions** (purpose): Factual / Experiential / Working
- **Dynamics** (lifecycle): Formation / Evolution / Retrieval

merken positions as: **Token-level × Experiential × {Formation,
Evolution, Retrieval}** — i.e. it lives entirely in the
token-level storage dimension, is primarily experiential (events
the agent lived), and covers all three lifecycle dynamics
(remember→consolidate→forget). Parametric memory (tuning LLM
weights) and Latent memory (continuous hidden states) are
explicit non-goals.

### Relevance to merken

**Cherry-pick source, not a method.** The list has dozens of
papers — most of them are irrelevant or overlapping. Three
titles from the first 20 stand out as potentially worth a deep
read:

1. **"Pre-Storage Reasoning for Episodic Memory"** — directly
   relevant. Same theme as A-MAC: reason about the event
   *before* it enters storage, not after.
2. **"Hindsight is 20/20: Agent Memory with Retention and
   Reflection"** — the "retention and reflection" framing could
   inform how merken decides whether to consolidate vs forget.
3. **"CAM: Constructivist View of Agentic Memory"** —
   philosophical framing, worth reading for vocabulary.

**Action item:** when doing Phase B (LoCoMo runner), browse the
Evolution > Consolidation section of this list for other
numbers to cite in the benchmark table. The list is gold for
pulling "what's been published" claims into merken's RESULTS.md

## Cross-cutting findings

### 1. Memory admission as a structured decision is an active research thread

A-MAC + the "Pre-Storage Reasoning" paper + implicit theme in
the Du survey all argue the same thing merken argues: deciding
what to remember is its own problem, not a byproduct of LLM
generation. **merken is not alone, and it's not obsolete.**

### 2. LoCoMo is the benchmark we should add next

Three concrete numbers in the wild:

- A-MAC: F1 = 0.583 (hybrid)
- SuperLocalMemory: 70.4% (zero-LLM)
- TiMem: not yet verified, may be in the paper body

All published in 2026. LoCoMo is actively used. **Phase B of
BENCHMARK_STRATEGY.md just gained two competitor rows to cite.**

The judge-model question is still open (BENCHMARK_STRATEGY.md §8
open question #1). My lean is unchanged: Ollama-backed
llama3.1:8b-instruct as the default judge, user-overridable.

### 3. Content type prior is the one new decider idea worth trying

A-MAC's finding that content-type prior is the most influential
factor is the specific, actionable idea from this research.
merken's HeuristicWriteDecider currently treats all events as
equally admissible modulo length and duplication. A tag-based
content type prior would let:

- `tag=decision` → high prior, keep aggressively
- `tag=tool_echo` → low prior, skip unless novel
- `tag=ambient` → very low prior, skip silently

Implementable in ~30 lines of Python. **No LLM dependency.**
Gated on a loop_quality scenario that shows merken over-admits
ambient chat or under-admits decisions.

### 4. Ebbinghaus decay is a legitimate should_forget alternative

SuperLocalMemory's Ebbinghaus decay is the one mathematically
grounded alternative to merken's coverage-based forgetting.
Worth building as `EbbinghausDecayForget` if a longitudinal
scenario shows stale episodic accumulating.

Requires access to per-event access counts. vstash has them
internally (`chunks.access_count`, `last_accessed_at`) but
doesn't expose via DocumentInfo. **This would be another vstash
upstream ask** — related to but distinct from #172 and #173.

### 5. Temporal awareness is a gap in merken

CMA's temporal chaining and TiMem's hierarchical temporal
organization both point at something merken ignores: the
*order* in which events happened as a signal for recall. merken
stores `added_at` but doesn't use it. A `TemporalRecaller` that
re-ranks by recency on time-sensitive queries would be a small
addition to the should_recall family.

### 6. Trust-but-verify on second-hand summaries

Two of the six reading-list summaries had inaccuracies:

- A-MAC does **not** explicitly critique A-mem/Mem0 by name
- The Du survey's "episodic→semantic particularly underexplored"
  claim is **not verified** in the fetched content

This is not a criticism of the reading list — summaries drift.
But it's a reminder that when someone says "paper X says Y,"
the right response is to fetch and verify before committing Y
to merken's docs. **Silt's rule applies to literature, not just
data.**

## What this research does NOT change

- merken's current 4 decision primitives are still the right
  shape. None of the papers suggest a 5th primitive.
- merken's two-layer model (episodic/semantic) is not
  invalidated by TiMem's multi-level tree. Two layers work for
  the loop_quality scenarios and simplicity has non-trivial
  value.
- merken's local-first, zero-LLM-in-hot-path stance is
  actively supported by A-MAC (hybrid with ONE LLM call) and
  SuperLocalMemory (zero-LLM at 70.4% LoCoMo). We're on a
  defensible point in the design space.
- merken's CONSTITUTION.md §9 "empirical first" rule is
  reinforced. Every paper here reports a specific number on a
  specific benchmark. That's the discipline.
